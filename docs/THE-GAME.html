The game

Getting started · The tape · The code · Findings · Open questions

Demonia is a single-screen platform and exploration game. You start in a graveyard, out in the open — stars, grass, headstones and red earth — and go down into brick galleries with ladders, water and doors.

Everything below is measured against the binary. Anything that is a reading rather than a fact is said to be one.

Twenty-four screens and twenty-three maps

The table at 0xD000 has twenty-four entries, one per screen, each pointing at a compressed map in the hidden page. But the distinct maps are twenty-three: entries 13 and 14 both point at the same one, at 0x4D44.

Each map is stored with a run-length scheme that takes one line to read:

0xFF <tile> <how many>     repeat that tile that many times
any other byte             one tile

and decompressed it yields 640 tiles, that is 32 columns by 20 rows, which is the game screen. The decompressor is the routine at 0x819D and it leaves the map at 0x5480. From there, working out a cell is always the same:

0x5480 + row*32 + column

That twelve-instruction block turns up again and again throughout the listing. The five add hl,hl are the multiply by 32.

The maps run from 0x43DE to 0x5468 and chain with no byte of gap or overlap: each one ends exactly where the next begins. That is what proves the format has been read correctly, and there is a test for it.

The most expensive is screen 15's, at 347 compressed bytes; the cheapest is screen 2's, at 81. The more a tile repeats, the less the map costs.

Each screen's record

Behind the map table there are two more, and they are what makes each screen behave differently:

addresswhat it is
0xD000twenty-four pointers to each screen's compressed map
0xD030the behaviour table: twenty-four rows of four routine pointers
0xD0C0the records: twenty-four 88-byte records, one per screen

The behaviour table is this game's nearest thing to a script: up to four things that can happen on a screen. Unused slots are not left at zero, they are filled with 0xFD9A, the sound vector; that is, "nothing to do here" is written as a call to something harmless.

The records are recognisable because every one carries the marker FF <screen number in ASCII> FF at byte 84: the first at 0xD114 and the last at 0xD8FC, always 88 bytes apart. That marker is what made it possible to delimit them one by one.

And there sits one of the good things about this tape: the Loriciels monitor's command table and its mnemonic tables ended up buried underneath those records. That is in Findings.

What moves

The player lives in three bytes of the variable page: 0x8049 and 0x804A are X and Y, and 0x804B is the frame to draw.

Facing them are two kinds of enemy, and the code treats them separately:

  • The chaser (0x9E88) heads straight for the player and speeds up every four steps. 0x9638 compares the two positions with a thirty-two pixel window and, if they meet, wakes it up.
  • The creature, which has numbered states and moves between them (0x9975, 0x99C9, 0xADD8). In state 1, if it is not busy, it checks whether the player is within reach.

Then there is what falls (0xB61A), what enters from the side of the screen (0x9518, with a draw at 0x9E30) and the objects, which are drawn two cells by two (0xA60A).

The sprites

Sprite artwork is not stored loose: it goes in 33-byte records, 32 bytes of 16×16 pattern plus one of colour. The 33 is not a disassembler's choice, it comes from the ld de,0x0021 at 0x82A9; and the ld bc,0x0042 at 0x82AF says two records are read at a time.

There are two banks, and both counts come out exact: 242 records at 0x0000 and 214 at 0x1F32. The game switches bank by writing over itself: 0xABB3 writes into 0xAB1A, which is the operand of a ld c,n.

Screen and sound

The MSX1 cannot scroll in hardware, so the game does it by hand and carries one routine per direction (0x81CF onwards): drop a column, shift the rows, and the same the other way.

The scenery is 2,048 bytes of patterns at 0x6000 and another 2,048 of colour at 0x6800. The routine at 0x8164 uploads them to all three thirds of the screen — it saves HL with a push and restores it each time round — so tile N has the same artwork at the top, in the middle and at the bottom.

The music is four-byte steps: coarse and fine tone for channel B, and the same for channel C. The pointer starts at 0x7900 and, on reaching the 0xFF, rewinds to 0x79C8 and not to the beginning: the intro plays once and never comes back.

Demonia is a 1986 MSX game; its title screen reads DEMONIA and it is signed (C) 1986 PAR SABLATOU CLAUDE, and the tape is 58,334 bytes. That Microids published it comes from the catalogue, not from the binary. All rights in the game remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work.
